# Evaluation — PerCom working draft

We organize the evaluation around four questions that follow the VETS guarantee boundary.

## E1: Can Timeline express externally sourced reactive-temporal requests, and where are its boundaries?

**Design.** E1 is a boundary-table case study rather than a coverage ratio. We define the scope by user behavior: a single automation whose service calls (time, target, arguments, count, order) are determined by a device and clock input history. Within that scope we distinguish ten behavior elements (immediate reaction, delayed call, sustained condition with reset, one-shot with re-arming, stored-value flow, sequence and branching, repetition until a condition, fixed-count repetition, clock anchors and time-of-day, and time-versus-event races) and five boundary candidates (in-flight cancel/restart, independent flows or instances, look-back event memory, variable-interval repetition, and nested repetition). Requests that do not fix a call sequence (pure safety properties, cross-automation priority, fuzzy triggers, physical completion) are excluded from the denominator.

**Procedure.** For each source-verified request we fixed the interpretation, marked assumptions, and wrote the expected timed ACTION trace for two to five input histories *before* writing any IR; the case file was hashed at that point. We then wrote the Timeline encoding, submitted it to an independent semantic audit (preserved / preserved after fix / changed / omitted), and replayed every history on the reference IR runner with a 1 s tolerance, reporting exact agreement separately. Language expressibility, conformance to the documented NL-to-IR grammar, reference-runner support, Explorer outcome, binding feasibility, and JoI feasibility are recorded as separate columns, and none of the later columns can upgrade the language verdict. The runner, front end, and tolerance were pinned before the run and not changed to fit results. Our procedure follows the spirit of AutoTap's request classification and Dwyer et al.'s pattern matching, but those collections informed the design of their notations, so we do not cite them as held-out precedents. One AutoTap safety property was converted to an automation by the researchers and is marked as such.

**Corpus result.** Twelve requests drawn from Home Assistant blueprints and community threads, IFTTT applets reported in the trigger-action verification literature, and four trigger-action programming user studies covered all ten behavior elements and two of the five boundary candidates. All twelve were judged fully expressible: the front end accepted each encoding, the audit found the interpretation preserved, and all 41 histories agreed with the pre-registered traces, 41 of 41 exactly. Three encodings were corrected after the audit and the pre-fix results are retained: a zero inter-iteration period was needed for two event-driven loops whose bodies always block, and one clock-anchored case initially mistook an already-present occupant for an arrival. Twelve of twelve is a count within the selected cases and is not a coverage claim.

**Boundary probes.** Because a corpus of successes cannot locate a boundary, we separately pre-registered three requests that target the two boundary candidates the corpus did not reach, fixing their expected traces and hashing them before attempting any encoding. These probes are reported outside the corpus denominator, and we treat "expressible" as a legitimate probe outcome. Two event-memory requests — the unordered member of the sentence pair whose ordered member is in the corpus, and a community request to notify on motion only when the door opened within the previous ten minutes — turned out to be *expressible*: a polling flow that snapshots `Clock.Timestamp` when an event is observed and tests the elapsed time reproduced both requirements exactly on all eight histories. That verdict covers events the automation observes while running. Events that precede the automation's start are outside the execution model itself, which supplies only the current value of each input at time zero and cannot state that a door opened before then; a platform that stores per-entity change times independently of automations, as Home Assistant does, answers that question without the automation remembering anything. The third probe, a community request to repeat an action every *n* minutes where *n* is read from a user setting, is *not* expressible: every duration operand is resolved at compile time, so the encoding that states the requirement directly is refused by the reference runner, and the setting is continuous-valued, so enumerating the interval by branching is not finite either.

**Two boundaries, of different kinds.** The probes separate a language boundary from a verification boundary. Variable-cadence repetition is outside the language. Remembering an event seen during the run, by contrast, is inside the language and inside the reference runner but outside what the validator certifies: the Explorer fail-closes on the elapsed-time guard that makes the encoding work, and on the unbounded observable domain the other probe reads. Timeline therefore represented every in-scope corpus request and both event-memory probes, while variable-cadence repetition, pre-start history, and Explorer-certifiable event memory remain open. We also report, as a separate column, that four corpus encodings use `wait` timeouts, nested cycles, or a zero period, none of which appear in the grammar currently supplied to the generator; those encodings are valid Timeline that the present NL-to-IR path would not produce. Genuinely concurrent instances were not probed: single-threaded control flow is a stated consequence of the execution contract, and the one corpus case labeled with independent flows is a special case reduced to a single flow rather than evidence of concurrency support. **[FINAL SECTION WORDING AND FIGURE/TABLE SELECTION PENDING.]**

**Contract facts surfaced.** The study also fixed several semantics that the Timeline section must state: `cycle.period` is waited after the body completes rather than as a fixed cadence; an edge wait whose condition is already true fires immediately; edges occurring during another wait are not latched; cron anchors are erased by the runner, which executes one firing window; and an unread variable reads as null, a behavior the verification contract does not currently specify.

## E2: Does the validator implement the declared semantics faithfully?

We will compare Timeline and JoI execution and Explorer verdicts against an independent oracle on boundary cases and positive and negative program pairs. Fault families include missing or additional calls, timing, snapshot value, order, sustain reset, edge re-arming, and repetition state. Unsupported, runtime-error, incomplete, false-equivalence, and false-divergence outcomes will be reported separately. **[FROZEN E2 PROTOCOL AND RESULTS PENDING]**.

## E3: What happens on generated JoI candidates?

We will generate JoI candidates from frozen confirmed Timeline and binding inputs and report every request as certified equivalent, divergent, unsupported or refused, incomplete, generation failure, or preparation failure. Equivalent outcomes must satisfy the applicable horizon-free closure conditions; a finite-prefix agreement will not be promoted to a certificate. The current development corpus and earlier fixed-horizon runs will remain provenance records rather than final E3 evidence. **[FROZEN E3 GENERATION PROTOCOL, MODEL CONFIGURATION, AND RESULTS PENDING]**.

## E4: How much exploration does VETS require?

The final E4 will vary input domains, control-flow size, timers, cycles, and state while reporting states, transitions, completion class, wall time, peak memory, and each reduction mechanism. Comparisons will use the same execution and observation contract and distinguish horizon-free closure, divergence, refusal, and incomplete exploration. Transition-count reduction will not be presented as a runtime speedup unless wall-time and resource measurements support that claim. **[SYSTEMATIC SCALE GRID, ABLATIONS, AND HORIZON-FREE COST RESULTS PENDING]**.

E1 and E2 remain necessary before broader adequacy or semantic-fidelity claims, and development evaluations on familiar candidates remain separate from final evidence.
